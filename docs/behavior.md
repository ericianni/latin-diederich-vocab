# What Latin 1400 does

Latin 1400 is an offline app for Paul Diederich’s high-frequency Latin vocabulary. The study deck is about 1,470 words (1,518 Lodge lines, with 48 cross-references hidden). Glosses are US English. There is no account and no server. Progress stays on the device.

The core list is always included. One added pack can be on at a time. Today that choice is Classical or Church Latin.

The app shows one standard classical form (Allen and Greenough). A normal alternate spelling can be accepted when typed, and is then marked in amber: “Accepted alternate — standard form: X.” Charts, lessons, the cheat sheet, prompts, and cards show only the standard form. Grading is right or wrong only.

## Cards

Cards are Leitner flashcards. Mark each one Right or Wrong. There is no ease or confidence rating.

A card sits in a box from 0 to 7. Each right answer moves it up one box and spaces it out: 1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60, then 120 days. A wrong answer sends it back to the start, and it is due again the same day.

Latin → English and English → Latin are separate cards for the same word. The English → Latin card is scheduled for the next local day after the first right answer on Latin → English.

On an English → Latin card the learner may type the Latin. The placeholder says “Type Latin (optional).” Macrons are optional unless Require macrons is on in Settings. With that on, a missing or wrong macron counts as wrong.

The Cards page still shows the day’s flashcards (new words and reviews, or a “done for today” message), how many words have been started, and a quick quiz. The quiz does not change the review schedule. When the day’s cards are finished, the learner can ask for five more new words. The tab bar is the way to open Drills. Cards does not add a second link there.

## Today’s goals

On Cards, both before and after the day’s flashcards, a small card is headed “Today’s goals.” The first line is “New words: N / goal.” N is how many new flashcards were introduced today: the first time a new card is answered. The goal is the daily new-word setting plus any “learn 5 more” extras. The second line is “Drill answers: N / 20.”

Both lines show the real count past the goal, with a check once the count reaches the goal. Eight new words against a goal of 5 reads “8 / 5 ✓”. Twenty-seven drill answers reads “27 / 20 ✓”. Each line has its own bar, and the bar fills only up to the goal.

The Drills tab shows the same drill line under the stats, as “Daily goal:”, with its own bar capped at the goal. “Answered today” is that same real total.

Both counts are part of the saved progress. They reset at local midnight, together with the other daily counts.

## Learn

Learn is short grammar lessons. The classical set is 21 lessons. Tables show the one standard form. A quick check does not move a flashcard. “Practice this in Drills” writes the drill filters for that lesson.

With the Church pack on, Learn adds nine lessons from the Ordinary of the Mass, then seven from the basic prayers, then six short readings. The readings are not beginner Latin. They are from Augustine’s *Confessiones* and Thomas Aquinas’s *Summa theologiae*. Finishing a reading, including its quick check, only marks the lesson done. It does not move a Leitner card or mark a word as learned.

## Drills

Drills are free practice. There is no due pile and no daily cap on how many you may do. The modes are Type, Identify, and Mix.

The app asks more often about forms you miss, have not tried, or have not seen lately, then picks a word that has that form. A miss comes back a few questions later as the same form on a different word. Weak spots lists the lowest-accuracy forms. The filters (word type, mood, voice, tense, case, number, and the rest) are on the Drills tab.

A typed answer that the app marks wrong can be counted as correct. An accepted alternate uses the same amber note as on cards.

Some entries are not in form drills. A pronoun that is only one case of a drilled headword (such as *mē* or *mihi*) is skipped. Other paradigms the engine cannot build reliably are skipped too, and the word list says so.

## Sentences in drills

Sentences in drills stay off until the learner turns them on. When they are on, about one question in seven is a short sentence made from words already seen (a flashcard, or a word already met in a drill). A sentence is never followed by another sentence. If a sentence cannot be built, or it cannot match the current filters, that question is an ordinary drill item instead.

Helper words the learner has not studied yet, such as *in*, *sum*, and *mē*, show a short gloss. Prepositions, *egō*, *tū*, *nōs*, *vōs*, and *sum* are always allowed. *is* and *sē* join later, once enough content words have been seen. *sē* is only the clause’s own third-person object (or *sēcum* with a verb of carrying, bringing, or taking). *is* is not used as the subject, and not for the clause’s own subject.

The learner can translate, identify one word, or both, and can choose Latin → English, English → Latin, or both. The defaults are both question types, and Latin → English. Translate is right or wrong only. Identify records the asked form. A missed sentence is not asked again. Each sentence adds 1 to the day’s drill count.

Longer sentence patterns unlock as more content words have been seen. The builder only makes indicative present, imperfect, perfect, and future, and noun or adjective forms in the nominative, accusative, dative, and ablative. Church-only words are not in this builder.

A separate Sentences (beta) page is linked from About on the web. That page is not the sentences mixed into Drills.

## And…?

Some forms have more than one reading (*puellae* can be genitive or dative singular, or nominative plural). Any one reading counts as right. The first answer is the one that scores, and the one the Leitner card uses.

Ask “And…?” is on by default. After a right Identify answer, the app can ask for the other readings of that same word. Skipping costs nothing, and the follow-up does not change the schedule. The other choice is “Just show them.”

And…? across parts of speech is off by default. When it is on, a right answer can also ask about a different part of speech the learner has already studied in the active deck, such as the noun *amor* and the passive of *amō*. It does not add another dictionary word that only shares a spelling, a difference of gloss alone, a word not yet studied, or a Church-only word while that pack is off. Macrons still keep *rēgī* and *regī* apart.

## Cheat sheet

The cheat sheet is the endings tables in one place: the declensions and conjugations the drills use, the pronouns, and the main irregular verbs. It can be opened from a drill. Every cell shows the one standard form. Alternates are not listed there.

## Words

The word list can be sorted by frequency rank or A–Z in every pack.

Classical also offers “Diederich’s order (by topic),” which follows his sections.

With the Church pack on, that choice is “By topic / lesson.” Core words stay under Diederich’s topics. Church-only words follow in a Church Latin block, with the subheads Ordinary of the Mass, Prayers, and Readings (the three tiers, in id order). If the list was still set to Diederich’s order when the Church pack is on, it uses this topic sort.

On the frequency sort, Church-only words have no rank. They are grouped at the end under a “Church Latin” heading. Core words that also have no rank stay ahead of that heading. Church-only lemmas have no Diederich section. Opening them no longer crashes the list.

## Settings

Settings are on the About tab.

**Pack.** Classical or Church Latin. The core list is always on. The Church pack adds its own words, glosses, and lessons, and only one added pack is active. The choice is saved. An older file that had the Church Latin switch on becomes the Church pack; otherwise it becomes Classical, including on import. Cards and lesson marks are kept. Turning the Church pack off hides its extra words and does not delete their cards. A shared word still has one card. While the pack is on, a Church gloss (and the dictionary line, when the overlay has one) replaces the classical one on the card, in drills, Learn, the cheat sheet, and the word list.

New Church words use the same daily cap as classical words: one Church word after every two new classical words.

With the Church pack on, the six tabs use Church drawings: a Latin cross for Cards, an open missal for Learn, a votive candle for Drills, a chalice for the cheat sheet, a scroll for Words, and a Chi-Rho for About. The header beside the title uses that same Chi-Rho, at its existing size, in the season color. The tabs take the tab’s own color, so the active tab uses the season color and the others stay muted. Classical mode keeps its original icons. Changing the pack swaps them at once, without a reload. The parchment, the liturgical frame, and the fonts stay as they are.

Two settings appear only for the Church pack: optional memorial colors (the season’s color, or the saint’s color) and a pronunciation guide. The guide is text. There is no audio.

Also on this tab: new flashcard words per day (5, 10, or 20; the default is 10), macrons optional or required, Ask “And…?” or just show the other readings, and And…? across parts of speech. Progress can be exported, imported, or reset.

In the Church pack, a few classical words change the form that is shown. The feminine of *dexter* is *dextera* / *dexteram* (*dextra* / *dextram* remains the alternate). *Caelum* shows the masculine plural *caelī* / *caelōs* (*caela* stays accepted). Proper names are capitalized in every generated form; *deus* stays lowercase. *Eva* shows *Evae*; a typed *Hevae* is the same spelling, not an alternate. *exsul* and *exsilium* are the shown spellings; *exul* and *exilium* are accepted. *ad īnferōs* is the masculine plural of *īnferus*.

Test mode, opened from a word, tries the features on that word. Nothing is saved until you leave, and progress is restored.

## Credits and How it works

How it works is the same short text for every pack. The labels Cards, Learn, Drills, and Cheat sheet are bold.

- **Cards:** learn a few new words a day and review what’s due. Mark each one Right or Wrong. Right answers come back later and later; wrong ones come back soon.
- **Learn:** short grammar lessons.
- **Drills:** practice word forms anytime. The app asks more about the ones you miss.
- **Cheat sheet:** all the endings tables in one place.
- Progress stays on this device. No account is needed, and it works offline.

With the Church pack on, one more line is added, and the label is bold: Church Latin adds words and lessons from the Mass, prayers, and readings.

Credits always name Paul B. Diederich, *The Frequency of Latin Words and Their Endings* (1939), the Lodge edition compiled by Carolus Raeticus (Hiberna Caroli Raetici), glosses from Gonzalez Lodge (1922) and J. T. White (1904), the CC BY-SA 3.0 license, Eric Ianni, the GitHub source, and version 3.3.0.

The Church pack adds its own credit: the Ordinary of the Mass from the public-domain 1920 *Missale Romanum*, biblical lines from the Clementine Vulgate, the prayers in the traditional wording of the *Compendium of the Catechism* appendix (2005), Augustine’s *Confessiones* (Migne, 1841), Aquinas’s *Summa theologiae* (Leonine edition, 1888), a new literal English translation, and usage checked against Nunn (1922), Beeson (1925), and Harden (1921). That paragraph is not shown for the Classical pack. The older tier blurbs, the Muccigrosso note, and the DWF.csv line are not in Credits.

## Liturgical colors

With the Church pack on, the frame and the line under the title follow the US Roman (Ordinary Form) color and celebration for the local date. The calendar does not change progress or the word list.

Ascension and Corpus Christi are on Sunday. That is the practice of most US provinces, including the Diocese of Arlington. Optional memorials keep the season color unless the learner chooses the saint’s color in Settings.

## Offline

The app is a progressive web app. After it has been opened once, a service worker serves the cached files, so it works offline. The live site is https://ericianni.github.io/latin-diederich-vocab/. On a wide screen (720px and up) the layout is a centered column with a top or side navigation. The phone layout is the default.

## Android

A Capacitor wrapper is prepared for package `io.github.ericianni.latin1400`, version name 3.3-android-4, version code 4. The debug signing key stays on the persistent computer. A cloud build does not sign the app or publish a GitHub release. The copy bundled for Android can lag the website until it is rebuilt.

## Known gaps

- The sentence builder does not use Church-only words.
- Finishing a Church reading lesson does not start or move a flashcard.
- The Sentences (beta) page is separate from sentences inside Drills.
- And…? does not offer a different headword that only shares a spelling, an unseen word, or a Church-only word while that pack is off.
- A sentence is only built for indicative present, imperfect, perfect, and future, and for nominative, accusative, dative, and ablative. Anything else in the drill filters becomes an ordinary question.
- Some dictionary lines are left out of form drills. The word list says why.
- The Android bundle in the repo can be behind the live site.
